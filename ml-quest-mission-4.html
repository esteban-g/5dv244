🔎 The Clustering Lab

Find structure without being told what to look for.

So far, you have worked with supervised learning: the training examples came with information about what the correct output should be.

But what happens when we have data and no labels?

This is where unsupervised learning becomes useful. Instead of predicting known labels, the algorithm can search for structure in the data.

In this mission you will become the algorithm. Your task is to experiment with K-Means clustering and discover how changing the number of clusters changes the structure that the algorithm finds.

🧠 Before entering the Lab

K-Means attempts to divide observations into K clusters.

Assign each point to the nearest centroid

then update each centroid to the mean of its assigned points. The process repeats until the assignments and centroids stabilize.

The important question is: where does K come from?

The algorithm does not automatically know how many meaningful groups exist. The choice of K is therefore an important modelling decision.

🎓 Mission Complete

K-Means demonstrates an important difference between supervised and unsupervised learning. In supervised learning, the training data provide information about the desired output. In clustering, the algorithm instead searches for structure in the observations themselves.

But there is an important limitation: a cluster is not automatically a meaningful real-world category.

The interpretation of the clusters requires knowledge about the problem domain.

Final thought: If an algorithm discovers three clusters in a dataset, does that prove that the real world contains exactly three meaningful groups?

The Uncertainty Engine

Mission 1: Teaching an AI to reason with uncertainty

Your AI agent has already learned how to work with knowledge, logic, and planning.

But there is a problem.

The real world is rarely completely certain. Sensors can be noisy. Information can be incomplete. Events can have different probabilities.

Your first task is to activate the agent's Uncertainty Engine.

You will learn to distinguish between what an agent believes before receiving evidence and what it believes after receiving evidence.

🧠 Stage 1 — What does probability mean?

Your agent encounters an event whose outcome is uncertain. Instead of saying simply true or false, it assigns a probability between 0 and 1.

Think of probability as the agent's degree of belief about an uncertain event.

A probability of 0 means the event is considered impossible, while a probability of 1 means it is considered certain.
Your agent observes a fair coin. What is the probability that the next flip will be Heads?

🎯 Stage 2 — Prior probability

Before your agent receives new evidence, it already has some belief about how likely an event is.

This is its prior probability.

P(Event)

Let's see how the agent's belief changes when we change the assumptions about the world.

Your agent's current belief that it will rain is:
50%
What happens to the agent's belief if we increase the prior probability of rain?
Key idea: The prior represents what the agent believes about an event before considering new evidence.

🔍 Stage 3 — Evidence changes beliefs

Now your agent receives evidence.

Suppose two fair coins are flipped. Before seeing anything, what is the probability that both coins will be Heads?

P(Two Heads) = P(Heads₁) × P(Heads₂) = 0.5 × 0.5
What is the probability of getting two Heads?

🔎 Stage 4 — Conditional probability

Something important has changed.

Your agent has now observed that the first coin landed Heads.

It no longer needs to reason about both coin flips. It already has evidence about the first one.

Conditional probability expresses the probability of an event given some evidence.
P(A | B)
Given that the first coin is Heads, what is the probability that the second coin is also Heads?
Notice the difference:

Before evidence: probability of two Heads = 0.25

After observing the first Heads: probability of another Heads = 0.5

The evidence changes the question the agent is answering.

It then receives evidence that makes rain more likely.

After considering the evidence, its belief becomes 70%.

  • Probability can represent uncertainty about an event.

Mission 4: From observation to intervention

You have learned how Bayesian networks allow an agent to reason about uncertain variables and observed evidence.

But there is a crucial difference between asking "What happens when I observe X?" and asking "What happens if I change X?".

Welcome to the Causal Intervention Lab.

Your mission is to discover why correlation is not enough when an intelligent agent wants to reason about interventions.

🔬 Experiment 1 — Observation or intervention?

Consider the following simplified situation.

Suppose that exercising regularly is associated with better health.

A simplified probabilistic structure
🏃 Exercise
→
❤️ Health

👀 Observation

You observe that a person exercises regularly.

Question:

What can we infer about their health?

🧪 Intervention

You deliberately make a person exercise regularly.

Question:

What happens to their health?

Why are these questions not necessarily equivalent?

🔧 Experiment 3 — Break the mechanism

In a causal model, variables can be connected through mechanisms.

An intervention changes the value of a variable rather than merely observing the value that naturally occurred.

Before intervention

→

Now imagine that an intelligent agent directly sets Cooling = ON.

The agent is no longer simply observing what happened. It is changing part of the system.

🧠 Causal Reasoning Reflection

You have now seen an important distinction in reasoning under uncertainty.

Observation asks what is likely given what we observe.

Intervention asks what happens when we deliberately change part of the system.

Causal networks are designed to support this kind of causal reasoning, going beyond mere correlation.
